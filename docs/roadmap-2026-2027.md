# Roadmap: September 2026 to August 2027

## Where we are

The web app is in a good spot. Storefronts, custom domains, email flows, affiliates, shipping through per-seller Shippo accounts, subscriptions, herdshares, the Shopify importer, and the Wrangler self-host tier all work. We just finished a full agent-readiness pass: the MCP server, UCP endpoints, OpenAPI spec, and developer portal are cleaned up and verified live. The boring-but-important backlog (test suite hangs, Pro receipt delivery checks, per-country shipping settings) is tracked as tasks and keeps moving in the background.

Mobile is the big open front. Phase 1 shipped: sign-in, nsec create/import, email auth, and the first tabs (feed, listings, storefront). Phase 2 (seller foundation: Stripe Standard Connect, hardened shipping and order flows, route cleanup) is merged. Phase 3 (seller listing CRUD) is an open PR in the process of being merged: the Listings tab goes from read-only to a real seller inventory flow with create, edit, delete, status management, and image upload. Nothing is in users' hands yet. There is no buyer checkout on mobile, no push notifications, and no store presence.

The seller focus is widening too: from raw milk into local food sellers and local artisans generally. Raw milk stays the wedge, but every decision below assumes the broader local-seller audience. Naughty Goat Cajeta, already listed on the marketplace, is our own first-party brand (separate entity, same parent company), which makes it the natural test seller, demo account, and content subject.

Two big non-engineering projects sit on top of all this: the rename, and the UI/UX redesign from the Summer of Bitcoin intern proposal. A third runs alongside them: the regenerative farm we are building from the ground up. The farm is mostly a content and brand play, but it eats calendar time either way.

About a fifth of working time stays reserved for the bug backlog and tests, and the dates below already assume that.

## Phase 1: September to November 2026

Theme: finish what is in flight, pick the new name, turn the intern proposal into a real design system.

### Engineering

- Land mobile phase 3 (seller listing CRUD) and ship phases 2 and 3 together to TestFlight and Play internal testing. The open PR is sitting with merge conflicts, so step one is a clean rebase. Seller scope at that point: listing create/edit/delete with image upload, order inbox, order detail, shipping label purchase, and Stripe Connect onboarding return handling.
- Buyer checkout on mobile, Lightning and Cashu first. Card checkout can stay a webview handoff this phase.
- Per-country shipping settings for sellers. Buyers keep asking and it unblocks international sellers.
- Close out the agentic commerce leftovers from the audit: UCP checkout session completion, MCP write-tool polish, and the ops tasks around Pro receipts and scheduled-post failure recording.

### Rebrand

- Name shortlist by mid-September, with domain and handle availability checked early so the winner is actually usable.
- Domain purchase by end of October. Do not announce anything before this clears.
- Quiet prep work: redirect map for every public route, NIP-05 plan, app store listing copy, email sender domain plan. All of it sits in a doc until launch day.

### Redesign

- Take the intern's proposal and break it into tokens, components, and page templates. Deliverable is a written spec plus a Figma (or equivalent) file, not code yet. Two weeks in October.
- Component audit of the web app against the new spec. This tells us the real cost of the redesign before we commit to dates.

### Milestones

- Oct 15: new name chosen and domains parked.
- Nov 15: mobile with full seller listing management in external TestFlight, at least 5 real sellers using it.
- Nov 30: redesign spec signed off, component audit done.

## Phase 2: December 2026 to February 2027

Theme: public rebrand, redesign goes live on web, mobile hits the stores.

### Rebrand launch

- Target: first week of January. New year, new name, clean story.
- Cutover checklist: 301s from milk.market for every route (kept forever), NIP-05 migration notes for users, structured data and llms.txt updated to the new brand, SendGrid sender domains re-authenticated, MCP/UCP docs and the developer portal updated, app store listings resubmitted.
- The launch runs as a two-week announcement campaign: Blog, Nostr, X, Instagram YouTube explainer, and direct outreach to every active seller before the public post.

### Redesign on web

- Roll out in layers: design tokens and HeroUI theme first, then shared components, then page templates. Marketplace and homepage first, seller dashboard second, storefront themes last.
- Storefront themes need care. Sellers set their own colors and fonts, so the redesign has to wrap their choices instead of fighting them. Budget real time for this; it runs into February.

### Mobile

- Public launch on both stores by end of January, buyer-focused. Feed, listings, storefront view, Lightning/Cashu checkout, order tracking.
- Push notifications for order events. Sellers getting a ping when an order lands is the single most requested mobile feature.

### Milestones

- Jan 8: rebrand live, redirects verified, zero SEO regressions after two weeks.
- Jan 31: mobile app public on App Store and Google Play.
- Feb 28: redesign live on all platform pages. Seller storefronts may still be in progress, that is fine.

## Phase 3: March to May 2027

Theme: seller parity on mobile, and the farmers market push.

### Engineering

- Seller tools reach parity on mobile beyond listings: inventory, discount codes, shipping defaults, order messages, and reviews. Listing management itself landed back in the fall, so this phase is about the depth around it.
- In-person selling mode. QR code checkout a seller can pull up at a farmers market stand, settling over Lightning. This is the feature that makes mobile matter for anyone selling at a booth or stall, and it is the demo we show at every event this year.
- Storefront theme migration finishes. Every seller storefront renders in the new design system without the seller touching anything.
- Agentic commerce v2: full UCP checkout flows end to end, MCP purchase tools documented with working examples, and a public agent starter repo. Agents buying from local farms sounds like a gimmick until a restaurant's procurement bot does it.

### Marketing

- Get 3 to 5 sellers running the in-person QR checkout at real farmers markets and craft fairs, and film it. This footage becomes the conference demo material in July.

### Milestones

- Mar 31: in-person QR checkout in beta with at least 3 sellers at real markets.
- Apr 30: QR checkout demo footage from at least 2 real markets published on YouTube.
- May 31: storefront theme migration complete, redesign fully done everywhere.

## Phase 4: June to August 2027

Theme: retention features and rounding out the year.

### Engineering

- Subscribe and Save on mobile, including managing an active subscription.
- Herdshare tools refresh: clearer member management, better share-status pages, and mobile access. Herdshares are our stickiest seller type.
- Reviews and trust improvements: verified-purchase review prompts over email and push, seller response support.
- Performance and stability quarter for mobile. Crash-free rate target 99.5%, cold start under 2 seconds on a mid-range Android.

### Marketing

- Summer content push tied to peak farmers market season (see schedule below).
- July 15 to 17: Bitcoin 2027 in Nashville. Main conference spend of the year. Booth plus live QR checkout demo on stage and at the table. The July date works in our favor: the in-person feature gets three extra months of real-market bake time before we show it to that crowd.
- August: no international travel this year. After Nashville, the rest of the summer goes to regional farm, homestead, and craft events close to home.

### Milestones

- Jun 30: subscriptions manageable end to end on mobile.
- Jul 17: Bitcoin 2027 Nashville executed, demo recorded, recap published within a week.
- Jul 31: herdshare refresh live.
- Aug 31: year-end review against this plan, next roadmap drafted.

## Content and event schedule

Cadence is the floor, not the ceiling.

### Recurring content

- YouTube: the primary channel, one video a week. Two series run in parallel: seller education (set up your storefront, take Lightning at a market stall, price a herdshare) and the build of our regenerative farm from the ground up. The farm series is the long-game audience builder, and Naughty Goat Cajeta shows up as the working example of a seller on the platform.
- Instagram: the focus for Naughty Goat brand awareness. Since it is acting as an independent product, the brand and its demand will be used to test reliability and quality of the software.
- Nostr: the brand npub posts at least three times a week. We are a Nostr company; our own presence has to be undeniable. Zap culture, reply to mentions, repost sellers.
- X: the second social home. Clips and stills from each YouTube upload, launch news, seller highlights. No original long-form there.
- Blog: two posts a month. Seller education first (how to price herdshares, shipping perishables, market-stall setup), then general guides for the site, plus regulatory help and news for local food sellers. The educational and regulatory posts double as our GEO/agent citability base, which the audit work this summer set up.
- Newsletter (beehiiv): deliberately not a focus. Monthly at most, product updates only, mostly repackaging what already shipped on the blog and YouTube.

## What we are explicitly not doing

- No marketplace-wide escrow or dispute system this year. Seller-managed refunds stay the model.
- No fiat-first pivot. Cards work because sellers need them, but Lightning and Cashu get the new features first.
- No second mobile codebase. Expo only, both platforms from apps/mobile.
- No rebrand halfway. Once the name is picked, everything moves. A lingering old logo anywhere is a bug.
- No events outside the US for now. Travel effort goes to depth at home.
- No newsletter-led marketing. YouTube, Instagram, Nostr, and X carry the audience work.

## Risks worth naming

- The rename touches SEO, domains, email deliverability, and app store listings all at once. The January date slips before quality slips on the cutover checklist.
- Redesign scope is the classic place roadmaps die. The Phase 1 component audit exists specifically so we find out the cost before we promise the dates.
- Mobile and web cannot both carry a big month at the same time. When in doubt, mobile wins through May, then web wins the summer.
- If the intern proposal turns out to need deeper changes than the audit shows, we cut page templates, not tokens or components. The system matters more than any single page.
